## The two halves of the thing you are installing

There is an application and there are browser extensions, and confusing them is the single most common
source of frustration in the first week. The application is the safe: your confidential material sits
inside it under strong encryption, and it is where entries are created, renamed and organised. The
extensions are the hands: they notice a sign-in field, recognise which site you are actually on, and offer
the matching entry. Neither half is much use alone. Installed together they cover the whole journey from a
credential existing to a credential arriving in the right box.

## What the encrypted store is allowed to hold

Three categories, not one. Passwords are the obvious tenant. Identities — the name, address and contact
details that registration pages demand — are the second, and they are what makes a fourteen-field sign-up
form finish in a click. Credit cards are the third, which turns a checkout screen into a confirmation
rather than a search through a wallet. All three live behind the same encryption and the same master
password, which is why the master password is the one secret worth choosing slowly.

Two behaviours run on top of that store. Automatic strong password generation invents credentials nobody
could plausibly guess, so a new account never has to reuse an old habit. Anti-phishing protection ties a
stored credential to the specific site it belongs to; present it with a convincing imitation and nothing
is offered. The autofill that quietly fails to happen is the warning.

## Building the store in the right order

Set the master password first, and treat it as the only thing you will ever memorise again — long, unique,
and genuinely recallable at seven in the morning. Add the extension for the browser you actually live in
next, not all four at once. Then stop trying to migrate anything.

The store is meant to fill itself. Over the following fortnight you will sign in to perhaps forty sites in
the ordinary course of work; save each one at the moment it happens and the collection assembles with no
dedicated effort. Attempting a single marathon import session is where most people abandon the exercise.
Only after a couple of weeks is it worth hunting for the accounts that never came up naturally, and by
then the habit is established.

## Practices that make it hold

Retire reused passwords in order of consequence rather than alphabetically: email first, because every
reset link on earth arrives there, then banking, then everything else. Let the generator produce the
replacements instead of improvising. Add the remaining extensions once the first one has proved itself —
Safari, Firefox, Chrome and Opera all read the same encrypted contents, so a credential created in one is
simply present in the others with nothing exported or pasted. And keep the build current: 8.12.34 is what
ships now, and security software is the wrong place to run something ancient.
